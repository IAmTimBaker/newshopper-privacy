NewsHopper Privacy Policy

NewsHopper is a Safari extension for macOS that opens web articles in the News app. This policy explains what information NewsHopper handles. In short: NewsHopper does not collect, store on any server, sell or share your personal data.

Information we collect

None. NewsHopper has no accounts, no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. It does not send any information to the developer or to any third-party service.

Information stored on your Mac

NewsHopper saves your settings, which are the list of sites you add and whether automatic opening is on, in Safari's extension storage on your Mac. This information never leaves your device. Removing a site from the list, or deleting NewsHopper, removes it.

Web pages you visit

To open articles automatically, NewsHopper checks the address of pages you open on the sites you've added. Safari lets NewsHopper see only those sites, and only after you allow it. These checks happen entirely on your Mac. NewsHopper does not keep a history of the pages you visit. It remembers an article's address for up to one minute so it can keep that article in Safari if you load it again, and then forgets it.

Opening articles in News

When you open an article in News, NewsHopper passes the article's address to the News app on your Mac through macOS's built-in "Open in News" feature. From then on, Apple's News app handles that article, and Apple's Privacy Policy applies.

Children's privacy

NewsHopper does not collect information from anyone, including children under 13.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
